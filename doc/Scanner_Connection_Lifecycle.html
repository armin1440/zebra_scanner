How a scanner joins, stays with, and leaves a phone

This report explains the connection rules of the plugin as ideas. It covers the first connection, reconnection, disconnection, and the guards that keep each scanner with its own phone.

Summary

Short answers

Connect, reconnect, disconnect

The phone shows a QR code that carries its own id. The scanner that reads it announces that id over Bluetooth. The phone connects only to a scanner that announces its id, and then saves that scanner.

After a lost link, the phone tries again with the saved scanner only, until it succeeds or the app stops it. Disconnect keeps the saved scanner. Forget erases it and makes a new id.

A scanner stays with its phone

The scanner has no list of allowed phones. The protection lives in the phones. A phone reaches only for a scanner that carries its own id, or for its own saved scanner. A neighbour phone never matches your scanner on either test, so it leaves your scanner alone.

A phone stays with its scanner

The phone looks for scanners only while it pairs or reconnects, and it accepts only its id or its saved scanner. While connected, it ignores every other scanner, closes any other link, and turns only the connected scanner's data into barcodes.

These rules prevent mix-ups between phones that run this plugin. They do not stop a deliberate attacker.

Vocabulary

Key ideas

Phone id
Eight hex characters, for example 7F3A0C91, made from random data once for each app installation. It stays the same until the app forgets the scanner. On Android the vendor library makes and stores it. On iOS the plugin makes it.
Pairing QR code
A scanner configuration barcode with two parts: "switch to BLE" and the phone id. A scanner that reads it goes into pairing mode and announces that id.
Saved scanner
The one scanner the phone remembers. Android remembers its Bluetooth address. iOS remembers the identifier that iOS gave it, plus its Bluetooth address. A new pairing replaces the record.
Owner
The scanner that is connected now. There is never more than one. Only data from the owner becomes barcodes in the app.
Two addresses
In pairing mode the scanner announces the phone id from a temporary address. In normal mode it uses its own address and announces its name and address, without the id.
Four states
Idle, Pairing, Reconnecting, Connected. The app gets an event on each change, and a separate "connected / not connected" event.

Lifecycle

Connect: the first pairing

A scanner joins a phone only through a deliberate act. The operator points the scanner at the QR code on that phone's screen. The steps below happen in this order.

  1. The app asks to pair. The phone checks that Bluetooth can work. iOSBluetooth must be on, allowed and supported. Otherwise pairing stops with an error. AndroidOn Android 11 and older, Location must be on for the Bluetooth search. If it is off, the phone opens the settings and pairing stops.
  2. The phone shows the QR code and starts to listen. The code carries the phone id. The phone listens for a scanner that announces this id. iOSThe search runs only while the app is in the foreground. It stops after 60 seconds by default. AndroidThe vendor library runs the search. The plugin sets no time limit of its own.
  3. The operator scans the QR code with the scanner. The scanner goes into BLE pairing mode and announces the phone id from a temporary address.
  4. The phone matches the id. Announcements with any other id, or with no id, are ignored.
  5. The phone chooses one scanner. Usually only one scanner read the code. iOSThe phone waits 1.5 seconds to collect every scanner that announces the id. The saved scanner wins if it is among them. Otherwise the strongest signal wins. AndroidThe vendor library chooses.
  6. The phone connects. iOSThe connection must succeed within 10 seconds. If it fails, the phone goes back to listening. AndroidThe vendor library connects to the temporary address, asks the scanner for its real address, tells it to leave pairing mode, and connects again on the real address. The app receives progress steps during this.
  7. The scanner becomes the owner and the saved scanner. Any older scanner is dropped and any reconnection to it stops. The app receives "connected". iOSRight after the connection, the phone asks the scanner for its real Bluetooth address and saves it. Reconnection needs it (see two addresses).

What happens to a scanner that is already connected?

Android drops it as soon as pairing starts, and stops any reconnection to it.

iOS keeps it connected during pairing. It is replaced only when a different scanner completes pairing. If the operator cancels pairing, the old scanner simply stays.

Lifecycle

Reconnect: back to the saved scanner, and only to it

What starts a reconnection

  • The app asks, usually when it starts. The plugin does not reconnect at launch on its own. The answer is "yes" if the scanner connects within the time limit (5 seconds by default), otherwise "no". After a "no", the plugin keeps trying in the background.
  • The link drops by accident: the operator walks out of range, the scanner goes to sleep, or metal shelving blocks the signal. The plugin starts again by itself, after 1 second on Android and at once on iOS.
  • Bluetooth comes back on. A scanner that was connected when Bluetooth went off becomes the target again.

Rules

  • Rule
    Saved scanner only. The phone never searches for "any scanner" during reconnection.
  • Rule
    One attempt at a time. If the app asks again, the new request waits for the same attempt. No second connection starts.
  • Rule
    It does not give up. Attempts continue until the scanner connects, or until the app disconnects, forgets the scanner, or starts pairing.
  • Rule
    No saved scanner, or Bluetooth off when the app asks: the answer is "no" at once, and nothing starts.

How the phone finds the saved scanner

Android connects straight to the saved Bluetooth address. If that fails, it tries again every 5 seconds while the app runs. If Bluetooth is off, it waits and checks again every 5 seconds.

iOS uses three ways, in Apple's recommended order:

  1. It asks iOS for the device it already knows by its identifier and leaves a standing connection request. iOS completes the request when the scanner announces itself, even with the app in the background and with no time limit.
  2. If iOS has forgotten the device (after a phone restart or a Bluetooth reset), it looks among the devices already connected to the phone.
  3. While the app is in the foreground, it searches the air. An announcement that contains the saved Bluetooth address becomes the new target. The search restarts every 30 seconds so that iOS reports devices again. After a failed attempt, it tries again after 2 seconds.

iOS treats the two addresses as two different devices. The first connection uses the temporary address, so the identifier iOS saves at that moment points at a device that soon disappears. For this reason the phone asks the scanner for its real address right after each connection. The reconnection search then recognises the scanner by that address. After the first successful reconnection, iOS saves the identifier of the real address, and later reconnections work directly, also in the background.

Android does not need this step. The vendor library already moves to the real address during pairing.

Strict mode (iOS only, optional)

In strict mode the phone reconnects only if the saved scanner also announces this phone's id. This stops a phone from taking back a scanner that another phone paired with. The cost is high with this scanner model: it announces the id only in pairing mode. In practice, strict mode means the operator scans the QR code again after every lost link.

Android has no strict mode. After pairing, the vendor library tells the scanner to clear the id, so there is nothing to check.

On purpose or by accident

The plugin must not reconnect after a disconnect that the app asked for. So it remembers every link it closes itself, and the close event of such a link does not start a reconnection. On Android, a close event from a scanner that is no longer the owner is ignored for the same reason.

Data in transit

When a link ends, a barcode that has only partly arrived is dropped. Open questions to the scanner (name, battery, firmware version) fail with "not connected".

Forget scanner on Android

The vendor library keeps its id in a file and also keeps a copy in memory. The plugin writes a new id to the file and tries to replace the copy in memory. If that works, the new id is active at once. If not, it becomes active after the next app start. Until then, a new pairing still shows the old id.

Isolation · question 1

How does the plugin keep a scanner from joining another phone?

The scanner does not choose its phone. Phones start connections, and the scanner answers. So the plugin protects a scanner by making every phone strict about which scanner it reaches for.

  • Guard
    The QR code belongs to one phone. A scanner announces only the id of the phone whose QR code it read. Other phones that are pairing at the same moment ignore that id.
  • Guard
    No "nearest scanner" rule. A phone never connects to a scanner because it is close or has a strong signal. iOS versions up to 0.0.13 did this (any scanner with a name ending in "BLE"). Version 1.0.0 removed it.
  • Guard
    Reconnection is personal. A phone reconnects only to its own saved scanner. A neighbour phone has a different saved scanner, so it never reaches for yours.
  • Guard
    Pairing needs a physical act. The scanner must read a code on that phone's screen while the phone is in pairing mode.
  • Guard
    One saved scanner per phone. When a phone pairs a new scanner, it forgets the old record and stops reaching for the old scanner.
  • Gap
    Take-back. Scanner 1 moves from phone B to phone A by reading A's QR code. Phone B still has scanner 1 saved and keeps trying to reconnect. When scanner 1 is free (asleep, out of range), phone B can reach it first. To prevent this, forget the scanner on phone B at hand-over, or use strict mode on iOS.
  • Gap
    Other software. These rules hold only for phones that run this plugin version. A generic Bluetooth app, the vendor's own app, or an older plugin version can connect to any free scanner.

Isolation · question 2

How does the plugin keep a phone from connecting to another scanner?

The phone treats every scanner as a stranger unless it passes one of two tests: it carries the phone's id during pairing, or it is the saved scanner during reconnection. Everything else is ignored or closed.

  • Guard
    Pairing accepts only this phone's id. Announcements with another id, or with no id, are not candidates.
  • Guard
    Pairing has a short window. The phone listens only while it is pairing. On iOS the window closes after 60 seconds by default. The app should cancel pairing when the pairing screen closes. Outside the window, the phone ignores all id announcements.
  • Guard
    A clear choice if two scanners read the same code. On iOS, the saved scanner wins, otherwise the strongest signal, after a 1.5-second collection window.
  • Guard
    Reconnection only to the saved scanner. Android matches the saved address. iOS matches the saved identifier or the saved address in the announcement.
  • Guard
    Ownership lock. On iOS, the phone does not search at all while it is idle or connected, and it ignores what it hears. Any link that is not the pairing choice, the reconnection target or the owner is closed at once. A second scanner cannot replace the owner unless the operator pairs it.
  • Guard
    Data only from the owner. On both platforms, data from any other link is dropped. On Android, a scanner that turns up connected is accepted only if it is the saved scanner and pairing is not running. A reconnection that completes after it was cancelled is closed again.
  • Guard
    Forget makes old codes useless. A new id means that a scanner reading an old printed or photographed QR code never matches again.
  • Gap
    The id is not a secret. It is shown on screen and sent over the air in plain form, and it is only 4 bytes. It prevents accidents, not attacks. A person who copies it could make a device announce it while the phone is pairing.
  • Gap
    Same code, two scanners. If two scanners read the same QR code within the same 1.5 seconds on iOS, signal strength decides. On Android, the vendor library decides.
  • Gap
    Delayed id change on Android. If the in-memory id cannot be replaced, the old id stays active until the next app start (see Forget scanner on Android).

Open points

  • Check
    Confirm the address match on a device. iOS reconnection depends on the address that the scanner reports being the same as the address in its announcement. The iPad logs suggest this, but it is not confirmed yet.
  • Decide
    Hand-over policy. Choose how to close the take-back gap: forget the scanner on the old phone at every hand-over, or use strict mode on iOS and accept a QR scan after each lost link.
  • Decide
    Pairing time limit on Android. iOS closes the pairing window after 60 seconds. Android depends on the vendor library and on the app cancelling pairing.
